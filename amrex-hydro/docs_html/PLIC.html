Volume of fluid with PLIC

The PLIC directory provides geometric volume-of-fluid transport with a piecewise-linear interface calculation, for any AMReX-based solver. The interface in a cell is the plane \(\mathbf{m} \cdot \mathbf{x} = \alpha\) in unit-cube cell coordinates, with the liquid on the side \(\mathbf{m} \cdot \mathbf{x} \le \alpha\), and the plane is fixed by the volume fraction \(F\) of the cell through the analytic relations of Scardovelli & Zaleski (JCP 164, 2000).

Like Godunov, MOL and BDS, the library provides spatial operators and leaves the update of \(F\), its ghost cells and the synchronization of the levels to the solver. It requires Cartesian coordinates and does not support embedded boundaries; PLIC::CheckMesh aborts on either. The reconstruction is selected by name, as advection_type is in HydroUtils::ComputeFluxesOnBoxFromState.

What the solver provides

\(F\) needs PLIC::nGrowF(reconstruction) filled ghost cells. They should

  • use amrex::cell_cons_interp at coarse/fine boundaries, which keeps \(F\) in \([0,1]\);

  • be filled with the BCRec of \(F\), as for any other scalar, and then passed with that BCRec to PLIC::FillWallGhosts.

PLIC::FillWallGhosts reads the boundary condition of each domain face:

At a wall the extension gives a free contact line: the interface meets the wall at whatever angle it has, while extrapolated values would impose 90 degrees. At an outflow it continues the interface smoothly. The planes are extended beyond the wall cells within PLIC::wall_nsearch cells, along the face, of the interface, and beyond the others a ghost cell takes the value of its wall cell: at a shallow angle the extended plane would cross the ghost layers further from the contact line, where no reconstruction stencil of an interface cell reaches. A ghost value depends on cells further along the wall than one box holds, so FillWallGhosts works on a whole level, and its result does not depend on the box layout. Nor does it depend on the order of the faces: each reads no ghost cells of another, and a ghost cell beyond several (at an edge or a corner) takes the mean of their values, over those that extend a plane to it, which for a planar interface is the plane.

Projection

The solver owns the projection, for which PLIC::SharpFaceDensity gives a face density that balances gravity for a planar interface (Francois et al., JCP 213, 2006): with \(\rho_f = \rho_g + (\rho_l - \rho_g)\, \phi_f\), where \(\phi_f\) is the liquid fraction of the segment joining the cell centers on either side of the face, \(\rho_f \mathbf{g}\) is a discrete gradient. Its inverse is the face coefficient of Hydro::MacProjector.

Each phase density is a value or a cell-centered field (PLIC::PhaseDensity), for a low Mach solver whose gas density follows its temperature and composition. A field is the density of that phase, not the mixture density: the solver defines it in every cell, extended across the interface, with one filled ghost layer, and a face takes the arithmetic mean of the two cells beside it. A phase density that varies along gravity is then balanced exactly away from the interface, and to \(|\nabla\rho|\, h / 8\) on the faces the interface cuts. On several AMR levels it is called on each level; the projection averages the coefficients of a fine level down onto the coarse faces it covers.

Reconstructions

PLIC::FitPlanes fits the plane of each mixed cell, from the normal of the selected reconstruction and the volume fraction of the cell:

youngs

Youngs’ finite-difference normal.

myc

Mixed Youngs-Centered normal (Aulisa et al., JCP 225, 2007).

myc_height

The height-function normal along the dominant direction of the Mixed Youngs-Centered normal, which is exact for a planar interface, and the latter where the heights are not defined. The most accurate of the three.

On a structure one cell thick (a film, a gap, a filament or a drop), where the neighbors of a mixed cell on both sides along some line are pure in the same phase, the gradient of \(F\) is null or noise. There every reconstruction takes the principal direction of the Hessian of \(F\) instead, which lies across the structure.

The geometric kernels in hydro_plic_K.H and hydro_plic_normals_K.H derive from Kynema-SGF (formerly AMR-Wind), under its BSD 3-Clause License; the headers keep its copyright notice.

Adding a normal

A normal runs inside device kernels, so it is a template parameter rather than a virtual function. Write a struct in hydro_plic_normals_K.H, as described there, and give it a name in PLIC::dispatch_normal, which every operator of the library selects its normal with.

Verification

Tests/PLIC checks the plane geometry, the normals, the plane fit, the wall ghost cells and the face density to round-off. A run fails when its checks do not hold. See its README for the expected results.
